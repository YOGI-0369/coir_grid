Exposed soil can be difficult to protect while plants establish.

Rain, runoff and wind can disturb bare soil, especially on slopes, embankments and some riverbanks. A suitable erosion-control method may help protect the surface while vegetation takes hold.

Meet Coir Grid

Coir is a natural fibre from the outer husk of a coconut. Coir Grid is our proposed open mesh made from it. It is intended to cover exposed soil while allowing water and plants to pass through the openings.

Material

Coir fibre from coconut husks.

Form

Open grid mesh, or coir geotextile.

Purpose

Soil-surface protection, with support for vegetation establishment.

Status

Concept. Specifications will be confirmed through design, prototyping and testing.

How it works

  1. Prepare the site

    Assess the soil, slope, water flow and project requirements.

  2. Place and secure the grid

    Install the selected mesh using a method suited to the site and product specification.

  3. Support vegetation

    Let suitable plants grow through the openings, following the planting and maintenance plan.

Detailed installation instructions will be published once confirmed for the actual product.

Frequently asked questions

What is Coir Grid?

A proposed open mesh made from coir fibre, intended for soil-surface protection and erosion-control applications.

What is coir?

Fibre obtained from the outer husk of a coconut.

Where can it be used?

Possible uses include slopes, embankments and landscaping. Suitability should be assessed for each site.

Does it stop all erosion?

No. No erosion-control product is a guarantee. Results depend on soil, slope, water flow, product design, installation, vegetation and maintenance.

Can plants grow through it?

The open-grid concept is intended to leave room for vegetation. Growth depends on the site and planting plan.

How is it installed?

That depends on the final product specification and the site. Ask a qualified professional for project-specific guidance.

Can I buy it?

Not yet. Coir Grid is currently a concept.
